# P5B targeted literature matrix

Only primary papers, official proceedings, or publisher/author copies are listed. The literature supplies design principles, not evidence that a candidate transfers to medical data.

| Family / primary source | Problem | Core principle | Reusable here | Not novel for us | Difference from this setting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dykstra, “An Algorithm for Restricted Least Squares Regression,” JASA 78 (1983), [publisher](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1983.10477029) | Least-squares fitting under side constraints. | Project onto a constrained feasible set while minimizing distortion. | A pairwise order constraint can be treated as a minimum-distortion correction. | Projection onto convex constraints is established mathematics, not a P5 contribution. | Our scores are native patch logits, evidence is auxiliary/noisy, and deployment is a smoothing/interpolation operator. |
| Best & Chakravarti, “Active Set Algorithms for Isotonic Regression; A Unifying Framework,” Mathematical Programming 47 (1990), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01580873) | Weighted least-squares isotonic regression under order constraints. | Active-set/PAVA methods satisfy partial orders, potentially pooling violating items. | Explains why broader partial-order projection can propagate changes beyond the initiating pair. | Isotonic regression and pooling are not novel. | A broad feasible set is exactly the risk here; B3 shows score movement and deployment matter, and C1-like permutation is closed. |
| Dykstra & Boyle, “Alternating projections onto the intersection of translated convex cones,” ScienceDirect (1986), [publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0378375886901114) | Projection onto intersections of constrained convex sets. | Repeated projections can solve multiple constraints. | Makes conflict handling and repeated constraint application explicit. | Alternating projection itself is not novel. | Repeated projections could cascade across patches and recreate broad movement; no such procedure is authorized without a new guarantee. |
| Cheng et al., “Predicting Partial Orders: Ranking with Abstention,” ECML 2010, [author paper](https://weiweicheng.com/research/papers/cheng-ecml10a.pdf) | Predicting a partial order when pairwise relations are uncertain. | Abstain on uncertain comparisons instead of forcing a total ranking. | Supports pair-level acceptance/abstention rather than total E sorting. | Rejecting uncertain comparisons is established. | It predicts partial orders; we need a bounded intervention on an existing trustworthy Phase2B order and cannot use labels to calibrate abstention. |
| Hüllermeier et al., “Label Ranking with Partial Abstention based on Thresholded Probabilistic Models,” NeurIPS 2012, [official proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2012/hash/fe2d010308a6b3799a3d9c728ee74244-Abstract.html) | Label ranking with incomparable pairwise outcomes. | Pairwise evidence may be left incomparable when support is insufficient. | Reinforces pair-level acceptance/abstention. | Partial abstention is not novel. | Probabilistic thresholding would be a new calibration rule here; no threshold may be tuned from VisA test outcomes. |
| Geifman & El-Yaniv, “Selective Classification for Deep Neural Networks,” NeurIPS 2017, [official proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf-Abstract.html) | Classification with a reject option. | Trade coverage against selective risk by abstaining. | Provides the reliability rationale for doing nothing on uncertain evidence. | Reject options and coverage-risk curves are established. | It relies on a learned/calibrated selective predictor; P5B has no new training or test-threshold search. |
| Li, Clavette & He, “An Analytical Update Rule for General Policy Optimization,” ICML 2022, [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/li22d.html) | Stable policy updates under a constrained optimization view. | Restrict update magnitude to a trust region and solve a local update. | Motivates bounded authority and explicit action magnitude. | Trust-region updates are not novel. | The domain is policy optimization; here the object is a patch-score vector and deployment redistributes spatial effects. |
| Terpin et al., “Trust Region Policy Optimization with Optimal Transport Discrepancies,” NeurIPS 2022, [official proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/7d3298e48220b289318b533a848ea069-Abstract-Conference.html) | Policy optimization with a distributional trust region. | Bound distance between old and new policies. | Supports treating correction magnitude as a safety constraint. | Trust-region safety is not novel. | Their metric is not a validated spatial-support metric for this native grid; no spatial threshold is inferred here. |
| Keys, “Cubic Convolution Interpolation for Digital Image Processing,” IEEE (1981), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TASSP.1981.1163711) | Interpolation for sampled image signals. | Resampling redistributes local samples through an interpolation kernel. | Supports analyzing deployment as a spatial operator. | Interpolation kernels are established. | The exact path uses Gaussian blur and bilinear interpolation with align_corners=True, not the cubic kernel studied here. |
| Unser, “Splines: A Perfect Fit for Signal and Image Processing,” IEEE SPM (1999), [EPFL publication](https://bigwww.epfl.ch/publications/unser9902.html) | Mathematical foundations for interpolation and reconstruction. | Sampling/reconstruction choices shape spatial support and smoothness. | Motivates deployment-aware validation of native intervention. | Signal-processing interpolation analysis is not novel. | This is a patch-logit intervention with stage averaging and softmax; exact repository behavior must be checked. |
| Radford et al., “Learning Transferable Visual Models From Natural Language Supervision,” ICML 2021, [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/radford21a) | Zero-shot visual recognition through aligned image/text representations. | Relative visual-text representations can transfer without task-specific labels. | Supports preferring relative/feature evidence over industrial class rules for a future medical-transfer audit. | Zero-shot transfer is not novel. | CLIP transfer does not prove this adjudication rule is reliable on medical data. |
| Jeong et al., “WinCLIP: Zero-/Few-Shot Anomaly Classification and Segmentation,” CVPR 2023, [official CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Jeong_WinCLIP_Zero-Few-Shot_Anomaly_Classification_and_Segmentation_CVPR_2023_paper.html) | Zero-/few-shot anomaly localization. | Local windows and text prompts can support anomaly inference without conventional training. | Shows why same-image local/relative evidence is a plausible transfer primitive. | Zero/few-shot anomaly localization is not novel. | Industrial anomaly experiments do not validate a native score projection on medical images. |
| Zhou et al., “AnomalyCLIP: Object-agnostic Prompt Learning for Zero-shot Anomaly Detection,” ICLR 2024, [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=buC4E91xZE) | Object-agnostic zero-shot anomaly detection. | Learn object-agnostic prompts for anomaly semantics. | Highlights representation/semantic robustness over industrial class identifiers. | Prompt learning and zero-shot anomaly detection are not novel. | It uses learned prompts and does not establish that a P5B post-processing intervention transfers. |
| Wang et al., “MedCLIP: Contrastive Learning from Unpaired Medical Images and Text,” EMNLP 2022, [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.256/) | Medical image-text alignment with unpaired data and false-negative mitigation. | Medical semantics and negative construction differ from generic image-text training. | Warns that medical transfer must be frozen-tested separately. | Domain adaptation and contrastive learning are not novel. | P5B has no medical training, labels, or medical threshold calibration. |

## Review conclusion

The literature supports abstention on uncertain pair evidence, minimum-distortion rather than broad permutation, and validation under the actual spatial deployment operator. It does not provide the missing GT-free pair proposal for this repository, so it cannot by itself pass Gate 3.
